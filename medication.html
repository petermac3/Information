Heart Attack Medication

Lansoprazole
Taken before breakfast Many heart patients take medications that can irritate the stomach lining or prevent blood clotting. Lansoprazole belongs to a class of drugs called proton pump inhibitors (PPIs). It reduces the amount of acid your stomach makes, protecting against gastrointestinal bleeding that can be a side effect of aggressive heart therapies Side effects Usually mild including constipation, farting, dizzy or tired

Aspirin
Antiplatelet. Prevents blood clots Bisoprolol a cardioselective beta-blocker primarily used to treat high blood pressure (hypertension), chronic heart failure, and angina (heart-related chest pain). By blocking adrenaline, it slows the heart rate and decreases the heart's workload, which lowers the risk of heart attacks and stroke. Side effects. Dizziness,headaches,tiredness, cold fingers and toes.

Clopidogrel
An antiplatelet medication that prevents blood cells from sticking together, lowering the risk of dangerous blood clots. It is commonly prescribed to prevent heart attacks and strokes in patients with a history of cardiovascular disease, acute coronary syndrome (ACS), peripheral arterial disease, or those with recent coronary

Rampril
A prescription ACE inhibitor primarily used to treat high blood pressure, manage heart failure, and protect the heart and kidneys after a heart attack. It relaxes blood vessels, making it easier for the heart to pump and reducing the long-term risk of strokes, heart attacks, and kidney failure Side effects. Minor, including tiredness

Atorvastatin
Taken at Night Atorvastatin (commonly known by the brand name Lipitor). A prescription statin medication used to lower "bad" cholesterol and triglycerides in the blood, and to raise "good" cholesterol. It is primarily prescribed to prevent cardiovascular diseases, including heart attacks and strokes, in high-risk individuals. Side effects. Minor, including farting, constipation, runny nose.
